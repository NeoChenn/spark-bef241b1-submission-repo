Rig Rust Repair: a quadruped that finds and fixes pipe corrosion

Unitree Go2 + 0–0.45 m lift mast + UR3 arm on an offshore oil rig in Isaac Sim 6.2. It detects rust, sprays, soaks 30 s, scrubs with a PPO policy, verifies and paints, and every step is decided by what its camera sees.

Technical summary

User and problem

User: asset-integrity and maintenance crews on offshore platforms. Topside pipework corrodes all the time in salt spray. Today people find it from rope access or scaffolding near live hydrocarbons, then fix it by hand with remover, wire brush and paint.

Why it matters: the work is dangerous (height, weather, possible gas release), costly (every task needs a person flown offshore) and never finished (kilometres of pipe per platform keep corroding). A robot that patrols, judges and does the routine repair, and hands only the doubtful cases to a person, keeps people out of the hazard zone.

What it does

  • Perception decides the action. The rust heatmap is projected onto a (θ, z) grid on the pipe; that grid sets the spray path, scrub targets, paint path and when the repair is done.
  • Adapts mid-run. New rust during the soak or scrub → re-plan: spray the new patch, extend the soak. Weld bead → back off. Glare → second view. Lens mist → hold. Worker within 3 m → pause.
  • Grounded scrubbing. Archard-style wear (removal ~ force × speed), 4× faster on sprayed and soaked cells, so the chemistry step matters to the policy.
  • Auditable. Every transition logs detector confidence, rust fraction, patch ids and force to decisions.jsonl and the HUD.

System architecture

PATROL→DETECT⇄ SECOND_VIEW →APPROACH→SPRAY→SOAK 30 s(new patch ⇒ re-spray) →SCRUB (PPO)⇄ BACK_OFF →VERIFY(>5 % rust ⇒ scrub, ≤3 cycles) →PAINT→VERIFY_PAINT→DONE· any state → HOLD / PAUSE_PERSON / ESCALATE_TO_HUMAN

Critical failure modes → response (on the HUD)

Over-forceForce > 40 N → BACK_OFF: retract 5 cm, press × 0.7; 3 back-offs → ESCALATE. The policy is trained to stay under 40 N, and its setpoint is capped at 38 N outside the network.
Missed / residual rustPainting over rust is the costliest error. The threshold is set for recall ≥ 0.95 in both domains. VERIFY needs <5 % rust, ≥80 % of the target visible and ≤5 % uncertain cells. Gemini can veto paint. Still rusty after 3 cycles → ESCALATE_TO_HUMAN with the residual cells.
Ambiguous viewp in 0.35–0.65 → up to 2 SECOND_VIEWs, then Gemini, then a human.
OcclusionNo frame or a flat frame → HOLD; escalate after 5 s.
Person nearbyWorker < 3 m → PAUSE_PERSON; resume when clear.

Safety design

  • Conservative by default: in doubt it looks again, holds or asks a person, and it never paints when unsure.
  • Hard limits live outside the learned policy: the 40 N gate, the 38 N clamp, the 3 m person radius, and caps on cycles, back-offs and holds.
  • The fast loop never waits on the cloud. With no key, network or valid reply, it runs exactly as it does without Gemini.
  • Escalations carry evidence (frame, confidence, residual cells), so a person can decide quickly.

Limits

  • Simulation only. The bridge poses the robot kinematically (FK/IK, not PhysX), the legs are fixed while scrubbing, and the brush force is an analytic spring, not a contact sensor.
  • The scrub policy trains on privileged grid state; in deployment, the localizer fills the grid.
  • There are only 13 sim-render test crops and no real painted-steel negatives. One real source has up to 13 % false positives on weathered surfaces.
  • The rusty deck grate reads as rust, so only pipe pixels are classified; the rest are masked grey.
  • The arm links do not collide with the pipe, and the IK does not hold the brush normal to the surface.
