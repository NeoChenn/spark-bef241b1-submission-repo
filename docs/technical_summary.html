Rig Robot: one quadruped, two jobs: rust repair and gas-leak response

Unitree Go2 + 0–0.45 m lift mast + UR3 arm on an offshore oil rig in Isaac Sim 6.2. One state machine, one camera, one arm, one decision log. Every step is decided by what it sees and senses.

Technical summary

User and problem

User: asset-integrity and maintenance crews on offshore platforms. Two chronic jobs send people into the hazard zone. Corrosion on topside pipework is found from rope access or scaffolding and fixed by hand. Small gas leaks at bolted flanges are found by a person carrying a gas detector, often right next to the leak.

The work is dangerous (height, weather, flammable gas), costly (people flown offshore for routine work) and never finished (kilometres of pipe and hundreds of flanges per platform). A robot that patrols, judges and does the routine fix, and hands only the doubtful or dangerous cases to a person, keeps people out of the hazard zone.

Two jobs, one platform

Rust repair. The detector heatmap is projected onto a (θ, z) grid on the pipe. The robot sprays, soaks 30 s, scrubs with a PPO policy (wear ~ force × speed, 4× faster once soaked), verifies with the detector and Gemini, then paints.
Gas-leak response. A gas check runs at every patrol stop and before every step. If gas is above 0.05 LEL, the robot walks to the nearest flange. A torque-stripe reader measures how far each bolt has turned and a classical crack check looks at the flange face. A loose bolt gets re-torqued and verified (stripe aligned, gas falling). A crack is never touched: the robot backs off and escalates.

Shared: gas pre-empts everything, even mid-scrub: the robot pauses the scrub, fixes the leak and resumes. Every transition, with its evidence, goes to decisions.jsonl and the live HUD.

Critical failure modes → response (on the HUD)

Paint over rustVERIFY needs < 5 % rust, ≥ 80 % of the target visible and ≤ 5 % uncertain cells. Gemini can veto. Still rusty after 3 cycles → ESCALATE with the residual cells.
Tighten a cracked flangeCrack p ≥ 0.5 → never a robot repair; back off 3 m, critical alert. While a crack is possible (unsure), no bolt is touched.
Gas at the limit≥ 0.20 LEL, or a failed sensor → GAS_RETREAT (fail-safe), then escalate. No tightening starts at ≥ 0.20 LEL.
Leak mid-rust-job> 0.05 LEL → pause the scrub, fix the leak, resume the same state.
Over-force> 40 N → BACK_OFF: retract 5 cm, press × 0.7; 3× → escalate. 38 N setpoint clamp outside the network.
Can't see wellUnsure rust or crack → up to 2 extra views, then a human. A dirty lens means “no crack” is not trusted. No frame → HOLD.
Person nearbyWorker < 3 m → PAUSE_PERSON; resume when clear.

Safety design and limits

  • Order of checks before every step: gas sensor working → gas below the limit → gas clear during rust work → person clear. The most severe hazard wins.
  • Hard limits live outside the learned policy (40 N gate, 38 N clamp, 3 m, 0.20 LEL, retry caps). In doubt, the robot looks again, holds or asks a person. The cloud is never in the fast loop.
  • Limits: simulation only. The robot is posed kinematically (not PhysX), the brush force is a spring model, and bolt torque and thread friction are not simulated. The gas model and thresholds are illustrative, not a site standard. The leak checks have not yet been re-tested on Isaac stream frames. The sim-render rust test is small (13 crops).
